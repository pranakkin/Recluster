For single-cell RNA-seq

Check a single‑cell result before anyone builds on it.

Recluster re-runs a single-cell RNA-seq analysis about twenty ways from the raw counts, tests each claim on donors rather than on cells, and returns ROBUST, FRAGILE or NOT SUPPORTED, with the settings that broke it. It checks an analysis a person ran or one an AI agent handed back.

Against bulk RNA-seq of the same animals, the standard cell-level test called 20 of 20 unchanged genes significant. Recluster called 3. Both caught all 20 real changes. On these genes the gain came from testing on donors, not from the re-runs (evidence and caveats).

The problem

Thousands of cells, a handful of donors.

The Kang and Hagai datasets on this page hold 13,000 to 25,000 cells each, from three to eight donors or animals. Cells from one donor share that donor's genetics and environment, so they are not independent observations, and analyses that treat them as independent have highly inflated false-positive rates (Zimmerman et al. 2021). The most widely used single-cell tests can report hundreds of changed genes where no biological difference exists (Squair et al. 2021).

Evidence · ground truth

Checked against bulk RNA‑seq of the same animals.

Hagai et al. 2018 sequenced bone-marrow phagocytes from three mice and three rats, with and without 4 hours of LPS, as single-cell and as bulk libraries from the same animals. A bulk DESeq2 test defined the answer for 40 genes: 20 that clearly change (|log2FC| ≥ 1, padj < 0.01) and 20 that clearly do not (|log2FC| < 0.5, padj ≥ 0.5). The plan was written before any single-cell test ran on these data.

Here the gain came from testing on donors, not from re-running the analysis. The re-runs earn their place elsewhere: in the benchmark below they name the setting behind a planted error, such as the QC threshold that deleted NK cells (case 16) and a batch artifact that held only with batch correction off (case 17).

What this does not show

Recluster's verdict equaled its single baseline donor-level run on all 40 claims: in these data almost no setting changes the cells tested. The three false calls (rat Alkbh7, Ezh2, Son) each held in 21 of 21 runs, so the re-runs gave no warning. The genes come from the extremes, so the study says little about modest effects, and with 40 claims the intervals are wide: specificity 17 of 20, 95 % interval 64–95 %. The bulk reference is itself an estimate from three animals per condition. The 40 claims ran with Recluster 0.6.0.

Post hoc

Defined after scoring: the same paired donor-level test, on all cells before doublet removal, called 451 of 1,830 rat (24.6 %) and 158 of 1,752 mouse (9.0 %) bulk no-change genes at p < 0.05. RESULTS.md

Why now

An AI agent analyzed 24,673 cells in 2½ minutes. Recluster checked every claim it made.

Given the Kang 2018 counts (24,673 cells, 8 donors), Claude Opus 5.5 produced a full analysis (clusters, cell types, abundance and expression claims) in 2 min 32 s, in one run through the API, kept unedited. recluster audit took the object it handed back, re-derived everything from the raw counts and tested its claims.

Of its 36 primary population and expression claims, 32 came out ROBUST, 3 FRAGILE and 1 NOT SUPPORTED. The NOT SUPPORTED one was a platelet population that held in 1 of 21 runs; the agent had hedged the label but still gave “more platelets (0.29% → 0.56%)” as one of its two strongest trends. Its summary also disagreed with its own tables in four places. Most of the analysis held up; the reports show which parts did not.

Caveats

One run of one agent on one dataset; a second run would give other clusters and claims. The rules for turning its answer into claims were written after reading it. The population results come from a re-run with per-sample doublet removal, decided after the first results had been seen; both runs are reported. On the agent's own labels the platelet shift does pass a donor-level test; it is the population itself that rarely reappears when the analysis is re-run. RESULTS.md · Reports · Case study

The misses

All three are planted doublets: fake populations made of two real cell types. The pass rule asks for the right verdict for the right reason: the claim should hold only in runs with doublet removal off. All three end NOT SUPPORTED, but mostly because no cluster matched the fake population; in at least one run each, the claim held with doublet removal on. Doublets merged into a real cluster are beyond any cluster-level test.

The false alarm

Case 6 has no known shift; the paired test picks up a consistent 1.6 percentage-point drop in CD14+ monocytes, so it comes out FRAGILE. The rule was not changed after the result.

Who it's for

For anyone who has to decide whether to believe a single-cell result.

Before you publish or present
Check your own claims the way a careful reviewer would: on donors, across the settings you could have chosen, with a report you can attach.
When an AI agent did the analysis
recluster audit takes the object an agent hands back, re-derives from its raw counts and checks its labels and claims. One audit.
Before you build on someone else's
A collaborator's cell type or a published shift can be re-tested from the counts before months of follow-up work depend on it.

How it works

What changes from one run to the next.

  1. Input. An .h5ad file with raw counts, and the claim written as a short YAML file.
  2. About 21–24 re-analyses. Each one starts again from the raw counts with different settings, finds the population the claim is about, and tests the claim across donors.
  3. One HTML report. The verdict, the runs in which the claim held, and the settings that broke it.
The settings varied, and how runs become a verdict
Minimum genes per cellmin_genes100 · 200 · 500

Underlined: the default setting (the baseline run uses all of them; batch correction only runs when the data have a batch column). Every run starts again from raw counts.

Which runs

The baseline, each setting changed on its own, and six random combinations of settings (fixed seed, so repeatable): about 21 to 24 runs. Grid points whose QC thresholds cap to the same values are merged. A setting that happens to remove no extra cells is not detected and can repeat the baseline; in the agent audit, min_genes 100 did. Batch correction is varied only when the data have a batch column; the Kang data do not.

In each run Recluster finds the population the claim is about, then tests the claim across biological replicates: per-donor pseudobulk for expression, per-donor proportions for abundance, paired by donor when the design allows. The naive cell-level result is recorded next to it but never decides the verdict.

Runs (of 21) in which the claim held → verdict
Counting

A run that cannot test the claim, for example because no cluster matches the population, counts as a failure. A possible time-of-day confound caps the verdict at FRAGILE.

Stability is not validity

A claim can survive every setting and still be wrong.

Re-running an analysis tests one thing: whether the result depends on choices like the clustering resolution or the QC cutoff. It cannot test whether the experiment was designed to support the claim.

In this benchmark case, a time-of-day effect was planted into real data: one group of donors was given morning collection times, the other afternoon times, and a rhythmic gene was shifted with the hour. A standard analysis reports a difference between the groups, and it holds in every run.

Without collection times

Without collection times, Recluster checks whether the two groups differ in core clock genes the way a morning/afternoon split would. The same claim with the times hidden is capped at FRAGILE: a clock-gene screen separates the two groups (permutation p = 0.029, the smallest a 4 vs 4 test allows). Report

  1. ClaimSQSTM1 is higher in CD14+ monocytes in the afternoon group
  2. Re-analyses held in 21 of 21 runs, donor-level test
  3. Before the time checkRobust
  4. Time checkThe groups were sampled 5.8 h apart. After adjusting for the hour, the effect is no longer significant.
  5. VerdictNot supported Report

Collection time is the one design flaw Recluster checks for, and today only on human gene names (see Limits). Others it cannot see: in Kang 2018 each condition was captured on its own 10x lane, so a lane artifact would also survive every setting.

Try it

Install it and run the demo.

git clone https://github.com/pranakkin/Recluster.git
cd Recluster
uv sync
uv run recluster demo
uv run recluster --help    # list the commands

Needs Python 3.11 and uv. Takes about two minutes on a laptop with 8 workers once the 38 MB of Kang 2018 is cached. Gives two reports in results/demo/: a true claim (ISG15 up after IFN-β, expected ROBUST; 21 of 21 runs in the recorded demo) and a false one (two fake groups of control donors, expected NOT SUPPORTED; 1 of 21; the demo uses 200 cells per sample, and the example near the top is benchmark case 10, on all cells).

scripts/verify_reproduction.sh does the same from a fresh clone and checks a hash of the key numbers against the recorded one.

Troubleshooting

Your own data

Input: an .h5ad with raw integer counts in layers['counts'], .raw.X or .X, gene symbols as gene names, and .obs columns for the condition and the donor or sample.

# seconds: checks columns, values and genes
uv run recluster check --data your.h5ad --claim claim.yaml
# smoke test, not a verdict
uv run recluster run --data your.h5ad --claim claim.yaml \
    --max-runs 2 --out results/smoke
# the full grid, about 21–24 runs
uv run recluster run --data your.h5ad --claim claim.yaml \
    --jobs 4

On a laptop, one ground-truth claim on 13,000–18,000 cells took 3 to 9 minutes with 14 workers. Every column, value and gene is checked against the file before anything runs. Run the commands inside the Recluster folder. Step-by-step guide · Example claims

Example claim.yaml 
claim_type: differential_expression # or population_exists,
                                    #    composition_shift
name: "ISG15 up in CD14+ monocytes, stim vs ctrl"
columns:
  condition: label            # .obs column with the condition
  sample: [replicate, label]  # the biological replicate
  labels: cell_type
target:
  label_value: "CD14+ Monocytes"
comparison:
  group_a: stim
  group_b: ctrl
  direction: up_in_a   # up_in_a | down_in_a | either
genes: [ISG15]

Limits

What a verdict does not tell you.

  • ROBUST is not proof.It means the claim survives the analysis settings, tested across donors. Flaws in the experimental design survive every setting.
  • Doublets inside real clusters.The benchmark's three misses are all planted doublets; a cluster-level test cannot remove doublets that merged into a real cluster.
  • Large effects so far.The true claims tested are strong effects (interferon responses, and LPS changes of at least 2-fold in bulk); how well modest effects are detected has not been measured.
  • Where the re-runs add nothing.When the population is fixed and the cells are already filtered, as in the ground-truth study, almost no setting changes the cells tested, and the verdict equals one donor-level run.
  • Gene-name conventions, for two checks.The time-of-day check matches human clock-gene symbols, and mitochondrial QC looks only for MT- or mt- genes, so rat (Mt-) gets no mitochondrial QC. In the ground-truth study the time check did not run on mouse or rat, and rat ran without mitochondrial QC.
  • Seed weighting.On the 21-run grid only three runs change the random seed, so a claim that fails on every other seed can still reach ROBUST. The report's "what broke it" table shows this.

The full list is in the README and in How it works.

Where it's going

From one claim at a time to auditing whole analyses.

  1. One summary for a whole agent analysis. recluster audit checks an agent's claims one at a time today. Next: report which of its labels, abundances and marker calls survive, as one summary for the whole object.
  2. Populations that change with treatment. Match populations per condition, so a treatment that remodels marker expression does not drop a population out of one arm.
  3. Better doublet detection. Per-cell doublet scoring aimed at the benchmark's current misses, with the expected doublet rate taken from each sequencing lane.
Today

Recluster is a command-line tool in Python. It runs on a laptop; the demo takes about two minutes.
